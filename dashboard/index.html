✳ FIRST, THE PHYSICS

A Higgs boson is too fleeting to photograph.

In a particle collider, protons meet at enormous energies. If a collision produces a Higgs boson, it decays almost instantly. Detectors capture the tracks and energy of its decay products; physicists infer which process most likely happened.

THE SETTING · 1 OF 3

Two protons meet. Their inner parts collide.

A proton is made of quarks and gluons. At the Large Hadron Collider, one of these constituents from each proton interacts. The experiment records the resulting spray of particles—not a photograph of a Higgs.
